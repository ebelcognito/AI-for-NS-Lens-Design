🎯 Zoom Out: The Project, The 5-Mirror Setup & What Qwen Did Executive Summary
1. What are we trying to achieve?
A high-resolution, compact off-axis freeform spectrometer (500–1100 nm, 10 mm slit, F/3.8, ~100 nm/mm dispersion) with diffraction-limited imaging (<2 µm spot).

Why not SFT/RL? SFT & RL are macro-explorers (generating rough numbers). They cannot solve the continuous, sub-micron physical aberration-balancing problem. What is missing is a high-precision physical refinement engine.
2. What is the problem with the 5-mirror setup?
To make the spectrometer compact (~100 cm³) and clear the entrance slit, 5 mirrors were added.

The Catch: 6 optical surfaces × high-order XY freeform polynomials created dozens of coupled, sensitive parameters. The standard optimizer (CMA / Powell) stalled at 4.5 µm RMS spot, unable to reach the diffraction-limited floor (<1–2 µm).
3. What did Qwen ACTUALLY do?
Qwen did NOT optimize the mirror shapes!

Instead, it ran a passive Monte Carlo tolerance & refocusing study: froze the existing layout, injected random 5 µm translation and 15 arcsec tilt errors across 200 trials, and tested a coarse 5-position detector focus shift (-100 to +100 µm). It screened assembly tolerance, not optical performance!
